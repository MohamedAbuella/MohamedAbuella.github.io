This project presents an open-source techno-economic optimization framework and educational case study evaluating multi-period capacity expansion, economies of scale, and operational decarbonization for regional biorefineries. In industrial energy and chemical systems planning, decision-makers face a critical trade-off between upfront capital economies of scale and recurring operational emissions liabilities under evolving climate policies.

The case study formulates a regional chemical manufacturing network across three candidate locations (Sites A, B, and C). It captures non-linear power-law capital cost degression (b = 0.70), strict standardized discrete sizing modules (Small: 30 kt/yr, Medium: 60 kt/yr, Large: 120 kt/yr), asset annualization via Capital Recovery Factors, analytical carbon price tipping points (0 – 300 €/t CO₂), and multi-period dynamic capacity expansion from 2020 to 2030 solved with merit-order economic dispatch.

Key Findings & Insights

  • Capital Scale vs. Variable Cost: In an untaxed baseline, Site A dominates the levelized production cost (157.78 €/t) despite having a higher variable cost, because deploying its maximum permitted Large class (120 kt/yr) leverages scale economies to dilute unit fixed capital charges to 67.78 €/t.
  • Carbon Price Tipping Points: Analytical crossover analysis identifies strict policy regimes: Site A is least-cost from 0 to 122.66 €/t CO₂; Site B dominates from 122.66 to 169.15 €/t CO₂; and the lowest-emission facility (Site C, 0.20 t CO₂/t) becomes the most cost-competitive option above 169.15 €/t CO₂.
  • 2030 Single-Site Deficit: A single facility cannot satisfy 2030 regional demand (110 kt/yr) because the system-wide maximum permitted plant size is Large at Site A (120 kt/yr), which yields an effective output of only 102 kt/yr at 85% availability—leaving an unavoidable 8 kt/yr deficit that necessitates multi-site deployment.
  • Optimal Multi-Period Expansion: Discrete combinatorial optimization across all feasible multi-period candidate paths proves that building Medium at Site A in 2020, upgrading Site A to Large in 2030, and adding a Small plant at Site B in 2030 yields the global least-cost expansion trajectory.
  • Average Cost Reduction: System-wide average production cost declines from 184.57 €/t in 2020 to 177.75 €/t in 2030 (−3.7%) because the Medium plant operates at partial load in 2020 (45 of 51 kt effective), whereas by 2030 the expanded Large plant runs at 100% baseload, spreading fixed capital charges over higher production volume.
